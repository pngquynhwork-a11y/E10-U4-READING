THE TEACHER BEHIND NEW YORK'S "FRIENDLY FRIDGES"

In early 2020, Selma Guzman, an elementary school teacher in the Bronx, New York, noticed a heartbreaking problem in her neighborhood. While many families were struggling to buy daily groceries due to job losses, local restaurants and markets were throwing away large amounts of unsold fresh food. Determined to help her community, Guzman decided to bring the "community fridge" movement to her own street.

Using her own savings, Guzman bought a used refrigerator, painted it bright colors, and plugged it in outside a local grocery store with the owner's permission. She stocked it with milk, eggs, fresh vegetables, and bread, placing a simple sign on the door: "Free Food - Take What You Need, Leave What You Can". Within hours, the fridge was empty, and neighbors began asking how they could contribute.

What started as a single sidewalk refrigerator quickly grew into a city-wide network. Local volunteers, high school students, and small business owners joined hands to set up dozens of "Friendly Fridges" across New York City. While Selma was teaching her online classes during the day, community volunteers were cleaning the fridges, checking expiration dates, and restocking shelves with donated produce. Today, her simple grassroots initiative feeds hundreds of disadvantaged families every week and inspires teenagers to take practical action in their own neighborhoods.
